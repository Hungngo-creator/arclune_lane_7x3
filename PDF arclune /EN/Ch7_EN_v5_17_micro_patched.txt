Chapter 7 — The Reflecting Citadel

Day 2

I opened my eyes in a round stone plaza inlaid with a single eye sigil. The light here had no source—just a thin film over everything. I stood before a gate: a black wall five men high, a faded inscription hung across its lintel. The chill didn’t come from wind but from the thoughts of the crowd pooling together. No aether here; the core furnaces slept like cold rock. Only Willforce threaded the air—thin, clinging. Raised letters: SAFE ZONE. A system panel flickered: “Timeflow: synced to Arclune (1:1). Daily quota: 3 entries.” I nodded—enough to hunt. Price boards floated all around: “Exchange Noetic Shards <-> shardlets (1:10).”

You could transfer balance through the Nameglass or cash out into physical shardlets; either way, handing someone shardlets was still just a transfer. The math sat plain; only hands and habits made it feel like coin.

“Hey! First time?” [UI: +1 query]
A short, round guy with a double chin and storm-cloud hair flashed a GUIDE badge. “Call me Bo Bo. Keep it tight and follow. Too many questions costs extra.”
“Where am I?” I asked him.

“The Noetic Realm—the world where minds gather.” He snapped his fingers; a fragment of the character “Idea” hopped up and dissolved. “Where there’s a lot of intent, Noetic Wraiths (Hu Linh) bloom. Humanoid, they talk, they have rules.”

“I asked for this citadel’s name, and I need the rules quick.”

He pointed under our feet. “Name’s the Reflecting Citadel, and three things a newbie needs.” Three fingers went up. “One, eat Noetic Shards to thicken your awareness. Two, you can also trade them for shardlets at the station. Three, don’t fight inside the blue ring.” He tapped the glowing rim around the plaza. “Through the gate, fate takes over. In here, everything’s priced.”

“What’s a Noetic Shard?” I asked.

“A piece of a *Hu Linh’s* body. They’re beings that arise where thoughts converge. The more people gather, the faster they spawn. Since they’re born from pooled awareness, shards boost Willforce—basically make your will/consciousness stronger. To keep *Hu Linh* from getting wiped out, they periodically split off bits of themselves into shards for safety. Players are happy with that—no slaughtering the goose for eggs.”

“Why don’t the *Hu Linh* fight back? They could kill players.” We walked as we talked.

“They can’t kill us all. People respawn; they don’t. And they’re… pragmatic—cut off some ‘flesh’ to buy peace. If they cross the line, the citadel shuts the gates.” We slipped into the flow. Feet never quite touched ground here, but you still felt tired if you thought “tired.” A hanging lightboard blinked a headline:

Noetic Bulletin: An Anonymous Engineer from the Starlight Junk Market filed a patent for nine-layer armor. Tagline: “If it can’t get you killed, then it’s worth the coin.”
Bo Bo lowered his voice. “Don’t stand near that kind.”

“Wasn’t planning to.”

A trinket stall sat between two lamp posts. The keeper’s hair was a nest; their belt sagged with junk so cheap it felt trustworthy.

“Treasure time! Anti-static whistle—one blow, your ears clear for half a second. Mist-lure incense—light it and packs slither out of their holes. Spotlight tag—shine it and stealth drops.”

“How much?”

“One Noetic Shard, level 1, per item.”

“Pricey.”

“Cheaper than a funeral.”

I took a whistle and a light tag. Paid with my thin Nameglass balance. [Nameglass: -20 shardlets]

-----

I handed Bo Bo five shardlets and got a honeycomb map. “Nearest mobs?”

Bo Bo pointed north. “Ash-stake field. Lots of Mist Wraiths. Tier 1—no speech; two common tricks: mist-form and a static-scream. Cooldowns are a few dozen seconds. Don’t stand still.”

I nodded and took the rental staff—a dark wooden rod tipped with a slim metal ring, shallow guide notches carved along the spine to keep your grip oriented. “Deposit?”

“Five shardlets. Extra if you scuff it.” He grinned, canine tilting like a comma off-center.

I stepped through the gate. Dry cold. Outside lay a lot of broken concrete posts, bare and splintered; silver fog crept the columns like living moss. Farther out stood hollow buildings, windows like sockets stripped of eyes. Fog rubbed at the ankles; the ground rang hollow under the posts.

A gray shape rose from a post’s base, lunging. A person-carved-of-mist: narrow shoulders, long arms, fingers smudged like smoke. No face; only a dark hollow.

It screamed. Not sound—dry vibration cracking across my head. Thought stumbled. I slid half a step back, staff rapped the ground for purchase.

It dissolved to mist, body fraying like a wrung cloth. Nothing solid to hit. I circled, kept my footwork beat. The fog knit—an opening showed at the “shoulder.” I thrust. The metal ring sliced cold; the mist-body stretched apart. It bled to vapor again. Wait. Another scream—I tucked my chin, teeth clenched so the inner letters wouldn’t slip.

On its third re-form, the blurry rim held a hair longer—a mist-form cooldown. I stepped straight in and cut across. The staff sheared through; the fog cracked like thin glass. It broke in wisps of hair-smoke and guttered out. Ash taste clung to the teeth; the lane pulled back by a thumb’s width.

A Noetic Shard dropped: a pearl of silvery glass no bigger than a fingertip, a coil of tiny script twisting inside. That was an *ideoglyph*—a note of leftover Willforce. Swallow it and the glyphs soak into your head; thinking gets sharper. I picked it up and put it in my mouth.

[Use Noetic Shard L1 +10 Willforce]
[Willforce: 40]

Warmth ran the spine; the outline of inner glyphs in my skull sharpened half a notch, the noise in thought thinning. No nausea—just thirst for another.

“Don’t gobble.” Bo Bo hollered from the gate, hands cupped like a megaphone, belly wobbling in the draft. “Too fast and the glyphs don’t dissolve—give it a minute or you’ll burp your *ideoglyphs* back up!”

I gave a thumbs-up. “One more.”

Four posts ahead smoked heavier—two Mist Wraiths crawled out. The left was short and broad, the shoulder edge like a lifter’s; the right taller, hands sharp as pen nibs.

The tall one screamed first. The short one mist-formed to flank. I leaned; the scream scraped my ear like ash shaken off. The staff swept for the tall one’s heel—slipped. No bones to hook.

The short one re-formed at my back—a mist palm clamped for my nape. I hunched a shoulder, stabbed the staff into the ground, stomped the metal ring as a pivot, twisted my body, and knocked the gray hand off-line. The tall one lunged—I raised the staff upright, caught it on the metal ring. Fog feathered apart in strands.

“Wait till it fully resolves!” Bo Bo barked. “Hit thick mist and you’re cutting smoke!”

A short breath. The short one had just finished mist-forming; its body hadn’t sealed, ripples still wrinkled the “chest.” I stepped on the diagonal, drew a straight line in my head so my hand wouldn’t shake, and drove the tip through the ripple. The fog hardened for a beat—then shattered. Second shard.

The tall one looked at me (if it had eyes) and screamed again—shorter, weaker. Cooldown not done. I pressed in, tapped three quick blows to “shoulder” and “neck.” It backed off, burst to fog to flee, but the neck didn’t turn in time. I cut across. Stillness.

I pocketed another, swallowed one, kept one. The letters felt clearer—tongue touching ink.

[Willforce: 50]

“Not bad.” Bo Bo waddled over, wheezing. “Why not cash out for shardlets and pace your meals?”

“I need the Willforce first.” I felt the spare shard through the cloth to make sure it was still there.

We went deeper. The posts gave way to open ground, a dry trench edged in needle-black grass; far off, a row of mildewed lamps pulsed a sickly green, marking a legal hunting zone. Bo Bo pointed at a collapsed frame house with smeared mist handprints on the wall. “Nest. See three or more—walk around.”

I nodded. From inside, a broad Mist Wraith heaved out—a rain-soaked cloak of a body, ragged hems, arms dragging to its knees. On its “chest,” a few shard chips glinted, like it had eaten but not digested. It screamed. The vibration hit twice as heavy. Bo Bo clutched his ears and squatted. My head went hollow a breath; vision swam. The big one shifted to mist—but slower, body too thick.

I pitched a pebble into the black hollow at its core. Reflex snapped it shut to “block”—that motion stretched the mist-form cooldown. I rushed, flipped my grip low, and rammed the metal ring into its “gut.” Rip. Half the upper body slumped like a raincoat pouring out. It tried to scream again—the note broke in the middle. I hammered once more. Stillness.

Three shards clinked down: one big, two small. The big one was old-coin-sized, script coiled thick inside—maybe from digesting kin.

Bo Bo’s eyes lit like he’d watched money fall. “That big one… thirty-something shardlets. Trade it?”

“Trade the two small, keep the big,” I said.

I angled for the deeper blocks. “Don’t,” Bo Bo warned low. “That’s the Mist Mother’s patch.”

But the fog had already braided. A woman-shape stepped out, arms long like willow boughs; rings of mist cuffed her wrists like silver.

She mist-swept past me—ice along the nape. Then split; four pups peeled off and crawled low like cats. One opened a maw and the vibration folded into the air—my head grew heavy, the letters in my mind slid out of place.

I blew the anti-static whistle. The shrill cut sideways; the pups stuttered half a beat. I pinned one with the staff’s ring, slung a second into an iron column—their fog spattered like salt rain. A third slid along my back; I turned the hips and let it miss.

“She’ll keep spawning,” Bo Bo yanked my sleeve. “Here it’s one more every minute.”

We ran. The Mother didn’t chase—she pooled by the nest, a rain-smell pressed flat.

“Tomorrow,” I said between strides.

“Bring more lights tomorrow,” Bo Bo puffed, palming his belly. “And less swagger.”

I nodded. “Where’s the exchange?”

“Inside the citadel. Or eat it. Dealer’s choice.”

“Back we go,” I said. “Lead.” He tugged me toward the station.

The exchange was a glass-tower kiosk with a girl rendered from light-strokes inside—cheery face, dead eyes. I set the two small shards on the tray. Letters scrolled; a soft “ting.”

Nameglass buzzed. [+20 shardlets]

“What about the big one?” Bo Bo glanced.

“Dinner. Tonight.”

He nodded and held out a palm for the staff. “Barely scuffed. Deposit returned.”

I passed the staff, took back five shardlets. “Is there a practice ring?”

“There’s a Civic Arena in the citadel. Rules are gentle—hit the red line, you lose. Winners earn bet slips; losers pay the venue fee.” Bo Bo winked. “Eat first. Going 0-for-tonight makes you a comedy act.”

I checked the schedule board: Same-day signup—1v1 trials (low factor). The cool from the shard still ribboned my gut. “Tomorrow.”

“Tomorrow it is. Tonight, skim the west field.” Bo Bo stroked his chin, pleased with himself. “Want to rent a mist-filter mask? Less headache when they scream.”

“How much?”

“Ten shardlets for the night. Pay tomorrow.” He handed over a matte-black mask, thin metal frame, tiny raised letters stamped on the surface.

I tried it on. New-leather smell. A dry bell clonked through the Safe Zone—dusk. The plaza lights stepped up a level; people streamed in and out like streaks.

“Food?” Bo Bo asked. “Place over there does *Yi Congee*. Settles the head when you swallow shards.”

“Lead.”

We sat under a hinged awning with bowls of gray porridge and faint steam. Faces around us came in all flavors: a silver-haired fox-eyed kid, a lanky guy with a bandolier of grenades… made of letters. Voices murmured; sometimes someone flashed a shard like a trophy.

“Listen,” Bo Bo said between sips, dropping his voice. “Past the post field there’s a tier-2 nest down by the south culvert. They set traps. Five tricks in rotation. Cooldowns are still cooldowns. Don’t let them drag the fight.”

“Got it.”

I finished and paid. Day’s net: roughly even on shardlets; shards left: 1 (large). I stood. “One more sweep. One more, then I’ll sleep.”

“Stay near the lamp posts.”

“Do you rent weapons?” He tossed me a flimsy cloth pouch. “Beginner’s practice knife, cloth-wrapped grip—don’t try to hack dense fog.”
[Nameglass: -2 shardlets gear fee; -5 shardlets knife deposit]
I nodded. Bo Bo waved me off and bragged to the next table about “a newbie who downed two shards without getting woozy.”

Night drenched the ground; fog clung low. Mildewed lights beat in pulsing circles. I stepped into the west lot. On a fallen wall, scratch-marks of mist crisscrossed like someone wrote in a hurry and erased it.

A tier-2 *Hu Linh* crawled out of a corner. Its body was firmer; half a face had knitted into a faint “mask.” The left hand bore a hook-blade like a fish’s fin; a hump of mist on its back, fist-sized—probably a reservoir for chained skills. It mouthed dummy syllables—a child’s voice cracking: “Go… home…”

It snapped the mist-hook; I rolled a shoulder. The rental staff hit like dead weight—no bones to bite. I popped the sheath catch and pulled the thin practice knife I’d borrowed—cloth-wrapped grip, stamped letters along the blade, a piece of intent-tooling for newcomers. The slim edge skimmed the hook; there was no clang—only the feeling of scraping across nothing.

It screamed—first pulse weak, second pulse right on its heels—double-skill. I backed three steps; the hook grazed my arm; cold flooded like anesthetic. My right pinky locked for half a beat—enough reminder that a slip here costs you letters and… face. I watched the hump on its back deflate—its mist battery dumping. A dry beat. I lunged, stabbed crosswise at the neck, then stomped on its shadow on the ground—the thing’s shadow was freckled like a sponge. It flailed, lost balance, couldn’t mist-form in time. The knife corkscrewed once more. Stillness.

Three small shards pattered down, almost clear, fine script looping around like sewn thread.

I swallowed all three. Warmth slid up; my head brightened a shade. The big one was for the night.

Back at the gate, Bo Bo waited. I returned the knife. He flicked the pouch’s seam and checked the edge.
[Nameglass: Deposit return +5 shardlets]
“Not bad.” He nodded, flashing the arena signup. “Slot at 19:00 in two days. Five-shardlet deposit today; safety check and weight class before the bout.”

“Name: Uyen.” [Nameglass: -5 shardlets]

I tipped my head at the Noetic sky—thick black, dusted with fine bright strokes like notation. Citadels around us blinked their lights in steady rhythm. In my pocket, the big shard chimed faintly with each breath.

The staff was gone but my arm still held the habit of bracing; I let it loosen and let Willforce settle like dust.

I tapped Exit. Counted down five beats. The Noetic night folded in.

Tomorrow, I eat the rest. Then the ring.
On the paving, a bead of light rolled like a needle’s eye and stopped exactly where I’d been standing.
