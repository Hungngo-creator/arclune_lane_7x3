Chapter 1 — Welcome to Arclune

Zhongzhou — Metropolis (Outer Districts — Violet-Lamp Alley)
6:00 a.m. The aether lines warm; the Web Array glows; the Name Shrine syncs. Violet-Lamp Alley hides under a steel viaduct; an aether train skims by, rivets ticking away. In this ring, any open display of power at Foundation or above is banned; those higher must wear an Order Seal. Flight is prohibited, except for maintenance drones and Warden kite-drones. Two- and three-story houses; windows face inward.

Morning begins with the breath of machine-worms under steel. Flaking paint. Steam beads at the sewer lips.

I jerk up from a pile of crates. Head splitting. I force down the original host’s memories: eighteen, orphan, begging to live. In my prior life I wrote web novels. Now I’ve crossed into a world of aether and arrays. At the mouth of the alley hangs a skewed wooden sign: “Name Shrine (Outer Districts) — Metropolis checkpoint.” Wind slides through; a bell tinkles. The city is hung with a Bell-Net: push aether past quota and the bell calls Wardens. If you want full‐on fighting—go to the arena.

A low voice plugs straight into my skull:

[Booting…]
[Host: 0 points.]
[Safety mode: lock all operations when combat arises.]
[Insight cut-scene runs only when safe.]
[0 points = erased from the world’s lettering.]

I hitch a smile. I tap it with a thought: “Hello?” No answer, but an interface is enough.

Instinct drives a deep breath. The air slips in like fine sand. A pale frame skims the edge of my vision:

[Skill: Breathwork — First-Tier Lv.1 (passive)]
Intake rate: roughly 5% of a basic reactor’s throughput.

Five percent—like a warm sip of tea that fades at the throat. The “reactor” here is a lot like the “spirit root” from the novels I read, probably named for how it bakes the flow.

I step out of the alley; the Outer ring opens. Steel spans like a dragon’s sinew; aether lamps mist violet. The well-to-do wear Name Mirrors — retinal rune‑lenses; most people keep a Nameglass (ID badge) at their hip. Far off, the Inner ring lifts glass blocks into the air.

Behind a tea counter, a woman in blue stands straight: cuffs slightly worn, hair braided high, eyes clear, voice cool and concise. On the counter lies a Nameglass—the handheld cousin of a Name Mirror; burnished bronze frames the corners; the pane is cold and dusted with pale metallic flecks. Touch wakes letters that rise like phosphor ink and dim with one’s breathing. A cultivator’s smartphone, I think.

Prices drift across the Nameglass:

Noetic Realm — a pure‑intent field: no souls, no flesh; only noetic imprints entry fee: Outer 3 L-stones · Metropolis 6 L-stones · Mechanica State/Capitals 9 L-stones
(Silver tier: pay per entry; Gold/Diamond: 3/5 free entries per day.)

High on a civic board a notice flashes gold: “Season 4 — Citizen Bracket open.”
Note: the Noetic Realm is an awareness-space; the body stays. Dying there more than three times a day can damage will.

My stomach growls. I have five shardlets in my pocket—poverty distilled. The frame in my head tints red:

[Mandatory task: Earn points]
[Points = survival.]
[Hint: farm the crowd’s emotions.]

Showboating for cash? Works.

Right at the counter, three tattooed men block the way: aether-scuffed leather, jaw-to-ear beards, triple-white eyes. The leader’s voice is rasp on wood.
“Protection fee, seventeen L-stones. Pay, or we ‘help’ you sell that Nameglass cheap.”

The stall keeper says evenly, “Name Shrine prices. I charge what’s listed.”

He sneers. “The Shrine doesn’t collect protection.”

I draw a breath, Breathwork opens my chest, and I step onto the stone riser.
“You’re full of killing intent but short on aesthetics. This city runs on law, not blades. In broad daylight, selling ‘protection’? Even birds would feel secondhand shame.”

Heads turn. The boss grates, “Who are you?”

“A letter-hand under the bell. No knife—just a pen.”

He slaps a sheet onto the counter. “Listed rate: five L-stones per day.”
The paper is crumpled: no Shrine stamp, no net code. I tap the lamppost under the bell beside the counter. “Public listing’s here, with a green seal.”

A yellow watch-light under the bell starts ticking; a lens slews our way—dispute-tracking mode. The woman in blue lowers her voice. “This alley charges per Name Shrine. There is no ‘protection.’”

His crew spreads to make a human gate. Some stalls pull tin curtains halfway; chatter drops until only the city’s machine-breathing remains. He grabs my collar and yanks me in; smoke and rust wash my face.

[Combat-lock engaged → interface operations frozen]

I roll my shoulder off his grip; touch my toe a half inch. Number Two lunges; I wrench his wrist and bump a knee with half strength—mostly for the crowd.

The woman in blue brushes her Nameglass, calling the Shrine. The lamppost sweeps a green circle; boot-heels ring in the alley. Wardens—Silver (Outer) arrive. The captain is early-thirties, stone-cut face, an Order Seal on his shoulder, the Outer badge cold.
“This street forbids drawn weapons. Want the blacklist?”

The leader shrugs. “Schooling a newcomer.”

[UI: +1 dislike]

I fold my hands. “Captain, they express affection with knives; I respond with words. Forty-odd witnesses—is that enough?”

He rakes them with a look. “Scram. Show up again within two blocks and your whole roost goes dark.” They scatter. Dust and mist settle.

I sit on the step; my legs tremble but my mouth still smiles. The interface flicks back:

[Balance: 98]

The woman in blue pours water, slides it over. “Drink.”

“My name’s Uyen,” my mouth says ahead of my brain.
“How should I address you… miss?”

“Tham Lang.”

“Got it, Tham Lang.” I nod. I glance at the Nameglass on her counter. “Do you rent Silver Nameglass?”

“We do.” She offers a Silver Nameglass with a few scuffs; the runes are still crisp. “Fifteen shardlets per day. Noetic entry here in the Metropolis is six L-stones per entry. The rest… depends on your letters and points.”

I cradle the thin pane, recalculating: a hundred shardlets make one L-stone. “So… six hundred shardlets a trip?”

“Outer’s cheaper: three L-stones.” She smooths her sleeve.

From the Outer ring we can still see a city-wide board at the Inner rim thump gold: “Noetic Realm — Season 4, Zhongzhou Metropolis — Citizen Arena.”

Comment scroll: “Outer is 3, Metropolis 6, Mechanica State 9—spicy.” I tap to hide it.

I look at my five shardlets and smile bitterly. The prompt drifts by in my head:

[Build an anonymous persona in the Noetic Realm → steady emotion farming.]
[Reminder: needs Nameglass (Silver or higher) + entry fee.]

I brace my palms. “Do you take IOUs?”

“No.” The corner of her mouth lifts just a hair.

[UI: +1 pleased]

“Then… trade services? I’ll write you a new sign for the stall. In exchange, seven days’ rent for that Silver Nameglass. If the sign tears, I’ll hang another—free.”

She is silent for three breaths, tapping a rhythm on wood. “You can write?”

“A little craft.” I smile.

She looks, a beat longer than a heartbeat. “Big name or big hand?”

“Sorry about the big name. The hand is average.”

A tiny nod. “Done.”

I borrow a fiber-optic brush, set out aether ink. As the tip touches, the crowd gathers itself, steam from hot tea rising. I write slowly on purpose, pulling eyes and breath to a single place. The first stroke flowers like an eye of storm; the second skims like a tongue of wind. As the ink dries, faint runic grain peeks and subsides.

In my head it’s just a tradesman’s habit: cadence of strokes herds cadence of sight; breath collected along the line. No magic—just showmanship.

“This lettering has qi,” someone says. “Has dao, too.” Another nods.
The crowd warms. [UI: +15 admiration]

I press a small seal into the wood grain—KLC—seed-small within the whorls. Tham Lang props her chin; her gaze softens. A kid hugging candied hawthorn peeks up. “Big sis, he writes so pretty.”

She rubs his hair. I wink; he grins with a gap. [UI: +1 fondness (kid)]

I lift the brush. The sign exhales. She receives it, voice low as wind between doorjambs. “Thank you.”

“My pleasure.” I wipe the brush and return it. “Now… to the renting…”

“I keep my word.” She sets the Silver Nameglass on the counter. “Seven days. Break it and compensate four L-stones. There’s a back storeroom—quiet, with a Nameglass console When you enter the Noetic Realm, set the Nameglass down, sign in anonymously, pay the fee.”

“Mn.” I nod.

The storeroom has a latch and a hand-bell inside. I test it—a grain-salt chime. Good enough to leave my body when I go under. It’s a compact room: table, chair, a small window toward a workshop. I latch the door. In my mind I call the System.

A dark cabinet opens mid-air like a medicine chest:

Might (First-Tier) — 1,000 points
Quickness (First-Tier) — 1,000 points
Sword Intent (First-Tier) — 1,000 points
…
Skill Point (First-Tier) — 100 points (spend to level one First-Tier skill you already own).

[UI: 120 points]

I shut it and step out.

“Going right now?” Tham Lang asks.

“Not yet.” I shake my head with a smile. “Still short six hundred shardlets.”

Her eyes smile. “Your letters are not bad at all.”

“I know.”

The Shrine bell trills out on the street. People part; a crew of toughs with blades tromps in. In front is Tran Quat—scarred brow, bristled hair, tall as a post, a char face and heavy eyes. Whispers: “They came back.”

“Careful,” Tham Lang says, cool as ever, hand on the Nameglass.

I step onto the stone as if to recite a poem.

Tran Quat about a foot away. Metal and opium cling to his leather. His voice thuds. “Earlier you got lucky with the Shrine. They’re not here now—keep talking.”

I tap the new sign I hung for her, turn back, and speak slowly:

“Line one: you slash, and the wind gets tired.
Line two: you glare, and the aether lamps go out.
Line three: you rage, and the Name Shrine rings the bell.”

The crowd ohs. Tran Quat’s shame flips to fury; he stabs straight. The interface slams down.

[Combat-lock]

I duck; the tip sings past my hair. A post splinters behind me. Not bad. But I need time, not victory.

“Name Shrine, heads up!” I pitch my voice like a market hawker. “Civic education show, free of charge!”

On the roof, a Shrine drone glides down like a mechanical moth; a blue dispute net climbs the air.

The street cracks up. His blade hiccups a beat. Boot-heels THUNK THUNK—the Wardens return, doubled. The same captain leads, eyes cold.

“Twice,” he says, like a winter wind. “Your whole roost goes red.”

Tran Quat swallows, glares long at me, sheaths his knife, and yanks his crew away. I rub my chest slow to bring the heart down. Civilian at heart; in the last life I wouldn’t kill a chicken.

[UI: +51 → Balance: 171]

As he passes, the captain tosses a line: “You talk fine. Next time, talk less.”

[UI: +1 admonition]

“I’m very calm,” I nod—still cheeky by a grain. He smirks and lets it go.

Tham Lang offers another cup. “Drink.”

I take it; her fingers are fine and warm. “Thanks. Could I crash here tonight?” She nods.

On the public board, a tiny article slides by:
“Outer-sphere literary circles debate the pen-name KLC Linesetter—rumor says a KLC seal has begun appearing on shop signs across the Metropolis.”

I fold the Nameglass. City lights unroll in bands; high aether lines breathe—like my ribs. From the Outer ring, panes on the Inner gleam like rainless crystal falling straight down.

[Newbie Objectives]

1. Amass 600 shardlets → first entry to the Noetic Realm (Metropolis: 6 L-stones).

2. Accrue 1,000 points → unlock the first skill.

3. Stay alive.

I grin at the sea of lamps. “If I die, it’ll be from zero points—not from a knife.”

The Shrine bell clinks. Down the block, the hawthorn-candy kid sprints past, glancing back; bright black eyes—he’d make a solid runner… remember him. Up on a high floor, someone reads “KLC Linesetter,” one corner of their mouth lifting.

I square my coat and stroll through the violet mist. Arclune, I’m here.
