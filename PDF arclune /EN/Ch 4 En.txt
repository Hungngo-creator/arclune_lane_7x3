CHAPTER 4 — The Price of a Letter, the Price of a Person

Evening purples the conduits, true to the alley’s name and to a city that believes its own poems. Garlic in hot oil meets the breath of sun-baked stone. Children pop up between adults like commas in a sentence that refuses a period. I’m under the arch again—arches like theater. I raise a Rate Board in my head:

Small board — 60–120;
Medium — 180–360;
Large — 600–900;
Patching — 30–90.

A stranger with a face I don’t like. Smile too perfect for his clothes. He takes up space in the crowd the way a fishhook takes space in water. He doesn’t make an offer; he lays out an absence. I know the shape of it: bait. I don’t bite.

I pick a medium face—an old, planed pine at a tool shop’s frontage—and write:

TRUE TOOLS
Borrow, buy, or mend.

Not elegant, just correct. Doesn’t need to be elegant.

Perfect-Smile stands still; the crowd doesn’t—flowing around him like a stream around a stone. He makes himself smaller and thus larger.

Wardens don’t stop by; they skim the alley-mouth like parents past a child faking sleep. The bell doesn’t ring. I write faster, then slower, then a hair faster again—because speed is a primer coat beneath whatever you let people see. I always keep a blank in my left hand; it keeps me honest—blanks demand thought, not flinch.

“Letter-boy,” a granny with hands the size of a wall calendar says, “my old man’s sign—back when he lived. The strokes broke apart. No one reads it when I tell them to read.”

“Bring it tomorrow,” I say. “I’ll rejoin the veins. Thirty to ninety shardlets. Or, if you prefer—”

“I prefer to cook noodles for you.”

“Then you cook noodles,” I say, voice flat.

The crowd laughs without cruelty. Perfect-Smile shifts his center, acknowledging the bit, but he doesn’t laugh.


---

Third pass at crowd-work. Honey-Lips reappears—speaks not to me but to Perfect-Smile. Good—information finding information saves me the introduction.

They stand just inside my sight so I can read the shape of their talk. Honey-Lips tilts his Nameglass; a flash for Perfect-Smile; then hides it. They part under the petty cover of bumping shoulders.

“Brother Uyen,” the youth slides back into the habit, “you sow a lot to reap little.”

“I like harvests that come back,” I say.

“And the Noetic Realm?” he says. “Three entries a day. You mean to go in a letterer, come out a letterer? You need a cover.” His eye glints. “A team.”

“You have a team?”

“Teams,” he presses. “People who know how to feed a scene.”

“I like people who know how to read a scene,” I say.

[UI: +2 provocation; +1 faint killing intent]

“I’ll float you one L-stone,” he says, sudden. “A loan. Pay me back after you win.”

“Loans are for people who can’t read odds,” I say, and because the effort deserves a tip, I gift him this: I look eight inches over his shoulder and… smile.

Two silver-shouldered wardens stand where seeing is effortless—at the alley’s head. They don’t need the bell; today they’re an audience. The youth’s body hears their bodies, and he backs two slow steps that try to look like a stretch and aren’t.

“You’ll ask again,” I say.

He smiles without admitting I’m right.


---

Time tilts the alley. Not much—just enough to nudge a hundred bodies into one point for one beat. I choose a mid-front with fresh, cocky plaster. I cut four big glyphs, brush reversed, then a third line you only see when you bow:

REPAIRS
SIGNBOARDS
done here

People bow. People breathe. A small quiet opens. The next sound feels like a whole room realizing they are in the same crowded room.

Tham lifts her palm—the global sign for don’t peacock. I nod without looking at her.

Perfect-Smile finally moves. He doesn’t step toward me but drifts toward the arch, where the yellow lamp under the bell just shifted its beat.
He raises a hand like a man about to grab the bell-rope—illegal, gets you flagged by the Name Shrine. He doesn’t touch it; he drops the possibility of touching it into the crowd’s mind.
I write on the blank:

THE BELL BELONGS TO ALL.
DON’T GRAB WHAT’S ALREADY RINGING.

He reads the letters and understands he’s been introduced—too publicly—to himself. The smile stays perfect; it just has to work harder to be true.

The post lamp catches, returns to baseline; the crowd exhales, remembering they’re on a street and not a battlefield.
A kid with sugar on his face raises his candied-haw skewer. “Mister, last one? Two shardlets only.”

“I’ll take the last,” I say, pay full price.
I flick the sugar shell—crack like thin glass; the skewer collapses neat in my palm into a pointer.


---

Night jokes with the alley and thinks better, leaving us in the long arms of dusk. A blue-jacket boy rolls in a drum: ink black like a moonless pond. Label hand-written mastic-rich black. Hua Du keeps time; his ink smells like intent.

“Where?” the boy asks.

“Inside,” I say. “Up against the back wall.” He shoulders it in and leaves a slip. I sign with one thrown stroke that makes him give a small snort.

“Nice letters,” he says, guarded.

“Tomorrow your noodles are better,” I say.

I seat the drum on the flattest patch of floor and pat it for its ring. It rings like money poured heavy.

I draw a breath; Linebound (System) dims up in my head—mist condensing on glass. I don’t speak. I don’t ever speak to it. Drawers slide out like a carpenter’s apology.

Breathwork (passive) — First-Tier Lv.1
Strength — First-Tier Lv.1 — 1,000 points
Quickness — First-Tier Lv.1 — 1,000 points
Sword Intent — First-Tier Lv.1 — 1,000 points
First-Tier Skill Points — 100 points/point

I rub the lip of the empty skill drawer—emptiness often sounds better than fullness—then close the Marketplace.

Outside, the alley starts rehearsing again. Perfect-Smile now has help: two women whose heads turn too often, and one kid who’s good at “I saw, I saw.” Their goal isn’t violence; it’s noise. Noise can drown delight and turn it to bile if left alone. I don’t.

I shift the stage three steps left, into the blink-strip of a conduit where the heartbeat is friendly. I carve a wedge of stillness out of air with posture alone. Then I run three small boards back-to-back like sliding beads.

Board one:
NEEDLE & THREAD
While-you-wait repairs. 60 shardlets.

Board two:
TEA
Light bitter, not harsh. 60 shardlets.

Board three (for the candle stall at the corner):
TRUE CANDLES
No smoke. Good light. 60 shardlets.

Order matters. You herd a crowd through three small doors and they forget they wanted to act up. Perfect-Smile tries to throw a shout; the shout hits three doors and turns into a polite cough.

Boot-heels clunk down the alley. Three strokes from the bell at the alley mouth—priority approach. Wardens arrive at a measured pace—the kind for when someone has just decided to be stupid but hasn’t had time to finish. A Nameglass lifts; it sweeps a face, drops to stamp an Order Seal, pauses; lifts again to catch a bystander’s profile.

Perfect-Smile decides he’s bored of his own plan. The crowd lets him withdraw with face shaved down by a few percentage points. I feel the subtraction like cool air.

---

Somewhere, someone plucks a thin chord—saved for tomorrow. “Close up. Sleep,” Tham says. I cap the ink tray, push it to the inner shelf; wind slips through the arch; the alley breathes even.

A bamboo curtain comes down; voices drain from the lane. Tham offers a gauze pad:
“Not deep. Tomorrow.”
I take it, hesitate. “Could you place it?”
“Sit still.” She leans in; two fingers lay the gauze across the burn; the warmth of her wrist trails the nape; tea on her skin. I swallow dry. “Done.” My heart falls back into beat. She re-fastens her cuff, slides a Nameglass unit into the drawer. I sip water to warm the throat.

“Am I pretty?” she asks.
“Like letters just wiped clean.”
“And you are… a bit bright.”
[UI: +1 amusement]
“How bright?”
“Enough that everyone knows who runs the stall.”
“Then haul the ink drum, stall-owner’s hands are tired.”

I lift the drum down. We tidy quick: boards into slots, wipe the tray lip, lid on, shelf it.
She sets a bowl in front of me. “Eat.”
I spoon once. “Good.”
“You can say that line every time I pay you.”
“Is there a surcharge for praising a beauty?”
“Yes. One more bowl.”
“Let the poor man eat first.”

She drops the curtain to a span; checks the counter. “Lock.”
I throw the bolt. Somewhere, someone plucks a thin chord—saved for tomorrow.

---

Night. I unroll a cot behind the tea counter. Bar across the door; “rent” paid in letters—patching a sign or rinsing the last pot at close. The storehouse is narrow and hot; I open the back, sit on the step under the viaduct for air.

The Nameglass panel blinks a brief:
[Core Continent — Last night, an unnamed thunder-cultivator used a self-devised method to shatter the Armor-Seven Souls of a Cloud-Sword Sect traitor atop White-Stone Bridge; target’s meridians and soul both cut before hitting the ground.]

In this city, weight isn’t owned only by wardens and bells, by seals and paper; it’s also the thing people do again and again—true enough to pass, and in front of enough eyes at once.

I call Linebound by intent. Drawers slide; I enter the Marketplace—three cards float beneath the glass:

Strength — First-Tier: stabilize bone/joint/tendon; small impact resistance; no swollen muscle, no change of frame.
Quickness — First-Tier: tune eye-hand axis, reduce step-lag and tremor; requires even breath base, adds tendon elasticity.
Sword Intent — Locked (minimum Genesis Stage 1 required).

Wallet icon grays: “Balance insufficient to unlock.”
I touch-hold Strength, leave a reminder like dust on glass, then close the Marketplace.

Above: steel girders; every aether freight that glides past makes the tin roofs shiver once, then hold.


---

I haven’t slept; head still full of wood grain and vowels. Tomorrow will collect—pay the mastic-ink debt with a large board in a place that deserves a large board. I have a man who seems to want to repay a favor. I have a drum in the corner like a promise.

Boot-heels at the alley head? Not tonight. Tonight the alley takes off its shoes, puts feet on cool stone.
From the direction of the Inner Ring, a hovering 3D banner washes the alley lip—Public Bracket logo spins once and dies when the wind turns; letters ghost on a puddle’s skin.

Tick. Something drops through the girders, skips by my foot: a thin bronzed ring, face etched with three parallel bars like a family mark. The inner edge is burnished with machine oil and aether soot; sized for a woman’s index or a narrow man. Still warm—fresh drop. Its clean glow cuts a tilted shadow of the arch—moneyed, and disciplined; the owner is near.
I wipe it dry, roll it once on the stone—the groove is nicked, small. I look up through the beams: a figure skims through, then nothing. I pocket the ring; it clicks faintly each breath. Back inside; bar the door.

The aether line along the girders breathes even, valves whispering like a loom. The night train goes light; tin roofs shiver once, then lie still.
