CHAPTER 2 — The Sign-Board Trade

Morning, Outer Districts of the Metropolis: the steel bridge overhead hums in a low register, aether conduits along its spine warming; dew pearls on rivets and ticks into a storm drain’s lid. The aether line has just switched shifts. I stand behind Tham Lang’s tea counter—her stall tucked right under the bridge span—leaning on the wooden door to watch the street. On the stone post at the alley mouth, the Name Shrine’s bronze bell gives a thin chime—like a spoon touching a cup rim.

A misted pane rises in my head like condensation on glass:

[UI: Host: 172 points.]

I ask the System with a thought: “What should I call you?”
A small voice answers: “Linebound.”

I breathe in. Lungs click cool. The breath is just enough to lift the air column a notch—about five percent of a basic reactor’s throughput.

Tham Lang brews the morning pot. Water touches tea with a sound hardly there. She glances at me. “Still dizzy?”

“Easing.” She reminds me, light: “Don’t go past the bridge foot this morning—the Inner Gate checkpoints are tight.” The Name Shrine’s lights are on a counting cycle; the post cameras sweep thicker than usual. Last night I finished the stall’s new sign: black field, silver letters, a tiny seal in the corner like a thread of dried ink. Passersby flick their eyes; each raised brow makes a tiny number pop up in my head, glint, and wink out.

“You still have your Nameglass?” she asks.

I tap my pocket—a thin pane answers. A headline flares: Sect Master of Cloud-Sword Sect duels stranger wielding famed sword Painted Skin—fight ends even! Comments flood the sky: “Sect Master’s fierce! To stand even against a famed blade!” “He’s mine aaaaa,” “Boss is insane,” “Sect Master…”

I fold it away. Gods trading blows have nothing to do with me. Six hundred shardlets for one entry—that’s the top novice target. For now it’s an empty stomach and a row of alleys that need letters.

“I’ll stop by Uncle Hao’s cobbler shop,” I say. “His board’s circuit snapped.”

She nods.


---

Uncle Hao’s place squats at the bridge foot, low roof, door paint flaking green; whenever the aether rail clatters overhead, the hanging rack trembles and ticks. The owner is thin, scalp close-cropped white, fingers drumming a sole.

I ask to use a plank. He squints. “You the kid who… wrote the board for that Tham girl yesterday?”

[UI: +1 inquiry]

“Yes.” I set the board down and pull a brush. “Let me try a sign for you. No charge. If anyone asks where to find ‘the lettering kid,’ point to this alley.”

He smiles; his eyes warm. “Deal.”

I breathe along the Breathwork—cool in the lungs, enough force to keep the wrist steady. The first stroke is a straight thread; the second curves so the straight won’t go stiff. The word Shoes grows—lifting the brush is the same as letting go.

I add a small line: Same-day resoling — foot measurement, hand-stitched insoles. Uncle Hao touches the board’s edge and exhales, pleased. Onlookers click their tongues. “Pretty letters. In the Inner they etch names with aether lasers,” he rasps a laugh, “but hand letters are warmer out here.”

“I can’t not pay you,” he rummages in a drawer.

“Save it for later. I need reputation.” He still stuffs forty shardlets into my pocket. I grimace a smile. No use playing saint; I need money. Points open skills. Money buys food and gear. Two different wallets.

Noon rain rakes the street. One board I just hung smears. I go back to fix it free, redrawing the lines, warning them if it blurs again, they’ll have to pay.

A young man steps in front of me: white teeth, smiling eyes, Nameglass dangling. He claps my shoulder. “Brother Uyen, watched your show yesterday. Loved it.”

[UI: +1 killing intent]

I smile vaguely. “You are?”

“A… friend of the block.” He wears a bent shield badge—the kind ‘self-appointed order-keepers’ like to flash. He leans close, voice syrup. “Do me a board sometime. And spare yourself the Name Shrine’s whining.”

[UI: +1 deceit]

I shift half an inch, smiling we’ll see. He leaves a cold stripe behind.

Toward day’s end, that “friend of the block” swings by the congee stall and slips coins over. “If you see that kid, no tab.” The lady nods. I look and swallow the joke in my throat.


---

The congee stall across the way is tin-roofed, low stools; steam coils up and the bridge’s damp iron scent hangs light. The owner—round, deft hands—scoops corn in quick strokes. She points at a weathered plank. “Can you fix this?”

I write “MORNING CONGEE” in round strokes, dotting a popcorn burst over the O; beneath: Just hot — properly seasoned.

“How much?” she asks.
“Sixty shardlets,” I say. She pays sixty. She hands me a warm towel. “Heard the Inner tightened gates last night—if you don’t have an Order Seal, don’t wander.”

Out here, anyone leaking aether from Foundation upward gets dragged in. The Order Seal—your gate pass, a moral leash for the strong—when worn, presses one’s aether down below Foundation. Body and reflexes no seal can chain. Someone tried a snatch-and-run once; didn’t even make it two stalls before a forearm was snapped—one arm done for. Here, the weak keep quiet; the world’s law is always the law of fists.

Two of Tran Quat’s old hangers-on loiter at the crowd’s rim. Their shirts no longer swagger; their eyes still sullen. The Name Shrine’s post light sweeps green; the bronze bell shifts half a tone; Silver (Outer) Wardens glint at the alley head. A passerby with an Order Seal strap glances over and taps his Nameglass; the two change their minds at once. A bridge worker mutters, “Silver coats are cranky this week—those ‘protection’ crews might be done.”

[UI: +4 resentment (cooling); +6 schadenfreude (crowd)]
[UI: +10 → Balance: 251]

I eat a comped bowl—seasoned right; a good woman, paying and feeding me both. The Shrine bell flips tone; a headline jumps: “TS-4 ‘Lan Tich Lake’—time dilation anomaly, exit temporarily sealed; Name Shrine raises alert, limits access.” No one reads it out loud, but the hurry leaks into the alley.


---

Afternoon purples the conduits. Back at the tea stall, Tham Lang says, “Someone asked for you.”
A middle-aged man in a tired suit offers a plank: “Bottle collector—small repairs.” I write it clean; he pays sixty shardlets.

“I’ll make another loop,” I say.
“Careful,” she tells me.

[UI: +1 reminder]

At the start of the auspicious hour, Wardens often swap shifts—ninety to one-twenty seconds. The Shrine flips up a ghost stop-line and broadcasts the change; the silver shoulder arrives a beat late.

One more loop brings me to a Name Mirror engraving kiosk; the craftsman with a loupe strap wants a small sign: Etch clean — etch straight — etch true. I write in one flow; he gives me a stamped voucher—free service at the Name Shrine.

The young man from the morning leans under a girder’s shadow, rolling an L-stone on his knuckle. “Want to build ‘reputation’ fast?”

[UI: +2 invitation; +1 killing intent (thin)]

“No need.” I tilt half a palm-width, slipping out of his body-swing angle in the dark. Right then another one leaps—blade tearing air. The Shrine post blinks a green tick; the lens aperture tightens half a stop. I sidestep half a step into the post lamp’s triangle; lift the board across my chest. CRACK—the plank doesn’t split clean but a fracture crawls.

[UI: Engagement detected → combat-lock]

The lens irises down another stop; the ghost stop-line spreads over tile like a ribbon of cold ink. The Shrine speaker says, “Outer Districts—maintain distance.” A small panel shows SHIFT SWAP ~90 seconds. No silver coat steps in during that window. The Wardens’ kite-drone slides a notch lower, lens blinking; if it catches your face, someone knocks your door tomorrow with a fine notice. They run. The board is done for.

The Shrine’s speaker recedes like water ebbing.

A kid selling candied haw thrusts a skewer. “Mister, buy one?”
“The smallest.” The thin sweet dissolves.


---

Back at the tea stall I slip into the rear store, slide the bolt, and call the board in my head—the System.

In my skull, a drawer of skills slides out like a medicine chest:

Sturdiness (Body) — First-Tier: 1000 points
Quickness (Body Step) — First-Tier: 1000 points
Sword Intent (Sensing) — First-Tier: 1000 points
First-Tier Skill Point — 100 points / 1 skill point

My finger skims the lip of the empty drawer. Two-ninety-seven—still far from a thousand. I shut the Store.

Out front, a voice rasps like sandpaper on wood: Tran Quat. He doesn’t yell; he speaks like a man swallowing anger: “That Tham girl… you protecting her?”

I open the door. He stands one step from the counter, knife at hip, hands empty. Two of his crew peek down the alley for patrol coats.

“Yesterday I spared you for the Name Shrine.” He twitches a smile. “Today we talk like men.”

I look at him and use my tool—metered lines as feints (a conman’s patter).

“First line,” I say slow, “you swing the knife—even wind gets tired.”
“Second: you glare—an aether lamp blinks.”
“Third: you growl—the Shrine taps its lens.”

The crowd lets out a low laugh. At the alley head, the bell goes… tink.

Laughter thins like foam; the numbers in my head pool and just brush three-thirty-something.

Tran Quat grinds his teeth; his hand kisses the hilt—click small but enough for the Shrine to tilt its eye. Silver coats spill in; he backs away, casting a look that promises later.

Tham Lang hands me a wet towel, saying nothing. I wipe my hands.

[UI: → Balance: 338]

The Nameglass pushes a hot note on TS-4 Lan Tich Lake: rumor says someone’s meddling with the dilation; first and second waves got pulled in and exits locked. On the side bar: in Sword Heaven someone surnamed Sword drops a line—only beating the victor of a fate-luck match is worth cheering.

That’s a sky elsewhere. In the Metropolis, I still walk. Up high, the Inner’s glass belt glints thin; in the Outer, engines answer with their steady thrum.

I kill the feed. Novice targets float up again:

1. Save 600 shardlets (6 L-stones) → enter the Noetic Realm for the first time (Metropolis rate: 6 L-stones per entry).


2. 1000 points → unlock the first skill.


3. Don’t die stupid.



Above, the aether lines breathe in even beats. I press my palm to the wood counter and feel the city’s pulse pass through the grain into my skin. Today I gathered 338 points—on letters and breath alone. But the bell about that secret-realm keeps tolling in my ear, and the thin killing glint in the morning punk’s eye hangs like a hook not yet pulled: small and weak not dying today means dying tomorrow. Letters can keep me fed; to go further, I’ll need more than letters.
